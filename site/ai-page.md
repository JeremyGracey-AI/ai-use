# How I use AI
<!-- Copy for jeremygracey.ai/ai — adapt headings/styling to the site rebuild -->

**AI-assisted, human-authored.**

Yes, AI is part of how I write. Teaching people to build with AI is my job — it would be odd if it wasn't. Here is the actual split:

**I build the thing first.** The architecture, the decisions, the opinions — those exist before any model sees them.

**AI drafts from my material.** My own notes, my past writing. It also helps me build diagrams.

**I edit until it sounds like me.** Usually a few passes. I read and approve everything before it goes out.

The projects are mine. The opinions are mine. If something is wrong, that is on me.

## The same standard, both directions

I build agent governance for a living — systems that check whether a deployed agent's actual behavior matches its declared behavior. This page is that standard pointed at myself: what I declare about my AI use should be checkable against what I actually ship.

Every project I publish carries an [AI-USE.md](https://github.com/JeremyGracey-AI/ai-use) declaring exactly what AI touched, what stayed human, how deeply it was reviewed, and who answers for errors. CI fails if the declaration is missing or stale.

## The written word, specifically

> I stand behind every idea and every sentence in every document. Anything I
> publish as writing — essays, posts, arguments, opinions — reflects my own
> thoughts, feelings, and beliefs. AI amplifies them. It does not originate
> them.
> 
> My thesis is simple: pen or speak it first. Prompt and outline it second.
> Then collaborate, iterate, and refine. AI is an extension of intellect, not
> a substitute for it — it aids me in thinking more clearly and more deeply,
> and in staying organized.

One extra rule for writing: the ideas have to be mine. AI can draft in my
voice from my notes and past writing. I review every draft, make my own
edits, and I'm the final judge.

What it actually does: agents scan social platforms and news feeds so I can
see what's surfacing and what's exhausted — the machine scans, I decide what
matters this week. I speak the first draft out loud with Wispr Flow, or Claude drafts
it in my voice from my notes, then I comb it together with my research notes. Then Claude peer-reviews — argues with
the structure, suggests edits — and I run a persona check, five imagined
readers from a near non-user to a hostile expert, to find where the piece
loses people. I take some of it and ignore plenty.
